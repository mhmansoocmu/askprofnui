TECHNOLOGY ACCEPTANCE MODEL — TAM (Fred Davis, 1989)

Core idea: Managers cannot get employees to use a system until they want to use it.

Two core constructs:
- Perceived Usefulness (PU): Belief that using the system improves job performance.
- Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU): Belief that using the system is effortless.

Extensions: TAM2 (social influence, cognitive instrumental processes), TAM3 (experience, anchoring).

Limitation for new phenomena: Designed for traditional workplace technology adoption. Virtual influencers may require social/hedonic models (UTAUT, HMSAM, CASA).

Key citations: Davis (1989); Davis, Bagozzi & Warshaw (1989); Venkatesh & Davis (2000) TAM2.

Scholarly search: "TAM" AND "technology adoption" AND [your technology/country].

---
UNIFIED THEORY OF ACCEPTANCE AND USE OF TECHNOLOGY — UTAUT (Venkatesh et al., 2003)

Integrates TAM, TPB, MPCU, IDT, and others into four core constructs:
1. Performance Expectancy (similar to perceived usefulness)
2. Effort Expectancy (similar to PEOU)
3. Social Influence (subjective norm, image)
4. Facilitating Conditions (organizational/technical support)

Moderators: age, gender, experience, voluntariness of use.

UTAUT2 (2012) adds hedonic motivation, price value, habit — relevant for consumer/social media contexts.

Scholarly use for virtual influencers: Social influence and hedonic motivation are especially relevant to why people follow VIs.

Key citation: Venkatesh, Morris, Davis & Davis (2003) MIS Quarterly.

---
HEDONIC-MOTIVATION SYSTEM ADOPTION MODEL — HMSAM (Lowry & Gaskin)

Focuses on hedonic (pleasure/fun) motivations for adopting information systems — beyond utilitarian TAM constructs.

Relevant for: social media, games, entertainment tech, virtual influencers where enjoyment and parasocial engagement drive adoption.

Scholarly search: "HMSAM" hedonic adoption; "hedonic motivation" virtual influencer.

---
TASK-TECHNOLOGY FIT — TTF (Goodhue & Thompson, 1995)

Adoption depends on fit between technology capabilities and task requirements. High fit → higher utilization and performance.

Use when analyzing whether a platform/technology matches what users need to accomplish (e.g., WeChat as super-app fit for Chinese collectivist, integrated lifestyle).

---
SOCIAL COGNITIVE THEORY (Bandura)

Key concepts: observational learning, self-efficacy, reciprocal determinism.

Applied to IT: Users adopt when they see others succeed and believe they can use the technology (self-efficacy). Relevant for influencer-driven adoption — users model behavior of influencers they follow.

---
DIFFUSION OF INNOVATIONS (Rogers, 2003)

Classic adoption curve: innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, laggards.

Five attributes affecting adoption rate: relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability.

Useful for country/industry case studies in final project and for comparing adoption speed across cultures.

---
COMPUTERS ARE SOCIAL ACTORS — CASA (Nass, Steuer & Tauber, 1994)

People apply social rules to computers and mediated agents. Human-computer interaction is fundamentally social.

Implication for virtual influencers: Social science theories (parasocial relationships, source credibility, social influence) apply to VI adoption — VIs are not "just technology" but social entities.

Key citation: Nass, Steuer & Tauber (1994) "Computers are social actors."

Scholarly search: "CASA" virtual agent; "computers are social actors" influencer.

APPLYING MODELS TO VIRTUAL INFLUENCERS (Assignment 2 Step 3)

Virtual Influencers (VIs): Non-human, digitally created characters sharing social media content to gain influential status among consumers.

Definitions (cite in assignments):
- Mouritzen et al. (2024): non-human digitally created characters engaging in interactive communications.
- Byun & Ahn (2023): digitized entities with anthropomorphized appearances, social roles, identity.
- Audrezet et al. (2025): digital personas via CGI and/or AI.

FIVE FACTORS students must identify from their framework — consider:
- Social influence / subjective norm
- Entertainment / hedonic value
- Perceived authenticity / anthropomorphism
- Visual appeal / parasocial interaction
- Platform context / facilitating conditions
- Cultural alignment
- Trust and credibility

Research findings (scholarly):
- Lee & Yuan (2023): female VIs, affect, disclosure, comment patterns.
- Sabie & Iancu (2025): gender portrayal differences in VIs.
- Miyake (2023): digital-orientalist commodification in VI representation.
