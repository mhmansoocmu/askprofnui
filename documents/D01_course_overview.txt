COURSE DESCRIPTION

This course prepares students to be effective digital transformation strategists. It adopts a global perspective, exploring multi-dimensional aspects of IS management including strategic leadership, cross-cultural management, and technology adoption and diffusion in different contexts.

The course provides understanding of IS-enabled change in the business environment, the alignment between IS strategies and business objectives, and ways in which businesses can leverage IS to transform and create digital strategies for sustained competitive advantage. Students are also exposed to digital ecosystems in various industries and across cultural platforms.

The course uses a flipped classroom model. Students pre-read each chapter before class, then contribute to discussions, activities, and assessments during class. Topics and schedules may be modified based on students' learning progress.

Textbook: Keri E. Pearlson, Carol S. Saunders, Dennis F. Galletta, "Managing and Using Information Systems: A Strategic Approach," 7th Edition, Wiley (ISBN: 978-1-119-56115-6). The textbook supplements class materials — students are responsible for independent reading.

---
LEARNING OBJECTIVES

By the end of this course, students will be able to:
1. Evaluate the global drivers behind digital transformation and their effect on society and organizations.
2. Identify the importance of aligning IS strategy with organizational objectives, and the importance of IS assets in supporting organizational success.
3. Identify factors involved in technology adoption and diffusion in different social, cultural, and organizational environments.
4. Analyze current and future technological trends and their implications on organizational direction for decision-making.
5. Analyze the key strategic issues facing management in deploying digital and IS strategies.
6. Formulate and recommend appropriate IS strategies to fit socio-cultural and socio-technical contexts and requirements.

CULTURAL AND TECHNOLOGY ADOPTION FRAMEWORKS ASSIGNMENTS

Students develop understanding of national cultural theories and the interplay between culture and technology adoption. Students research and present on cultural frameworks and IT adoption frameworks with examples from various cultures. There are 2 collections, each worth 20%.

---
FINAL PROJECT

Students complete a project on IT adoption in a technological and cultural context of their choice. The goal is to apply frameworks to analyze technology adoption, then recommend appropriate digital strategies to maximize benefits. Individual assignment, approximately 3,000 words.

---
SELF-REFLECTIVE ESSAY

Each student writes a 500-word reflective essay on their experience in the course — lessons learned and how the course applies to future careers.
